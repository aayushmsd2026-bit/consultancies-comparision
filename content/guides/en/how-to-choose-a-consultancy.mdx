A consultancy can save you months of confusion. A bad one can cost your family its savings and a year of your life. The difference is usually visible before you pay, if you know where to look.

You do not need to be an expert. You need to ask a few plain questions and watch how they are answered.

## 1. Check that it is registered

Education consultancies in Nepal need approval from the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology. Ask to see the approval letter. A real consultancy shows it without fuss.

Then check for yourself. Use our [registration checker](/check), or read [how to check if a consultancy is registered](/guides/how-to-check-registration).

## 2. Read what past students say

Do not rely on the testimonials on the consultancy's own website. Nobody publishes their own complaints.

Look for reviews on independent sites. Read the bad reviews first. One angry review means little. The same complaint from five different people means a lot.

> Ask the consultancy to connect you with two past students. Then call them.

## 3. Get every cost in writing

Before you pay anything, ask for one paper that lists every charge:

<Checklist>

- The consultancy's own service charge
- Fees that go to the college or university
- Visa and embassy fees
- Medical, courier, translation and document costs
- What is refunded, and what is not, if the visa is refused

</Checklist>

If they will not write it down, do not pay.

## 4. Ask who will handle your file

Meet the counsellor who will work on your application, not only the person at the front desk. Ask how many students they handle at one time, and how you will get updates.

## 5. Ask for more than one option

A good counsellor asks about your marks, your budget and your plans. Then they show you several colleges and explain the trade-offs.

Be careful with a counsellor who pushes one college very hard. Some colleges pay consultancies for every student they send. That is not illegal, but you should know whose interest the advice serves. You can ask directly: "Does this college pay you a commission?"

## 6. Keep your original documents

Give copies. Keep your original certificates and your passport with you until an embassy or a college truly needs them. Ask for a signed receipt for anything you hand over.

## 7. Take your time

<Callout tone="warn">

"Seats are filling up, pay today" is a sales line. A real deadline comes from the college or the embassy, in writing. You can check it yourself on their website.

</Callout>

Visit two or three consultancies before you choose. Take a parent or a friend. Sleep on it.

## The short version

- Registered, and you checked it yourself.
- Honest reviews from people you can reach.
- Every cost on paper.
- No promise of a visa. Nobody can promise that.
